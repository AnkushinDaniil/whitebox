The fastest way to understand Ethereum is to *re-invent* it. Not memorize its parts — derive them. Start with the simplest idea that almost works, find the one thing wrong with it, fix exactly that, and repeat. Do this eight times and you don't just *know* how Ethereum works; you understand why it *couldn't* work any other way.

This is the whole site in one arc. Each step here is a doorway into a deep, interactive explorable — but first, watch the design build itself.

<DepthLayer tier="intuition" title="Eight fixes from a shared ledger to a world computer">

## Build it up, one flaw at a time

You and a few friends are tired of settling cash. So you keep a shared ledger of
who paid whom. That's step one — and it's broken. Step through the fixes; at each
stage you'll see exactly what's wrong, and the smallest repair that fixes it.

<Callout type="insight" title="Nothing here was arbitrary">
Re-read the chain of fixes: signatures because anyone could forge a line;
balances because signatures alone don't stop overspending; decentralization
because one host is just a bank; consensus because decentralization invites
double-spends; the EVM because payments aren't programmable; gas because code can
loop forever; the state root because nodes must cheaply agree on the whole world.
Every famous piece of Ethereum is just the answer to "what broke in the last
step?"
</Callout>
