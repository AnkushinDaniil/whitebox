You've [built and signed a transaction](/how-transactions-work). Where does it go? Not into a block — not yet. It enters the **mempool**: a loosely-shared, per-node waiting room of pending transactions, gossiped peer to peer across the network until some proposer decides it's worth including.

Two questions decide its fate there: *will the network even relay it*, and *what will it cost*? Both come down to fees — and Ethereum's fee mechanism (EIP-1559) is one of the most elegant pieces of mechanism design in the protocol. It's not an auction. It's a thermostat.

<DepthLayer tier="intuition">

## A waiting room, and a thermostat

When you broadcast a transaction, your node validates it (good signature? nonce sane? can the sender afford it?) and, if so, **gossips** it to its peers, who gossip to theirs. Within a second or two it's in mempools across the globe. There's no single canonical mempool — each node keeps its own view — but they converge fast.

Now it waits, priced by two numbers you set:

- A <KeyTerm term="base fee" def="A per-gas price the protocol sets each block and burns. It rises when blocks are over half full and falls when under — a feedback controller, not an auction." /> you
  must at least cover. This is set *by the protocol*, the same for everyone in a
  block, and **burned** — it goes to no one.
- A **priority tip** you add on top, paid to the proposer, to compete for
  *ordering* and inclusion when blocks are full.

The clever part is the base fee. Before 2021, Ethereum used a blind first-price
auction: everyone guessed a gas price, overpaid out of fear, and fees were
chaotic. EIP-1559 replaced the guessing with a **controller**: each block nudges
the base fee up or down to steer the network toward blocks being **half full**.
Predictable, transparent, and — by burning the base fee — it ties ETH's value to
network usage.

## Make blocks full and watch the price climb

Each block can hold up to **2× the gas target** (the *elasticity*), but the fee
mechanism aims for blocks to sit right at the target — 50% full. Below, set how
full each block is and watch the base fee respond. Full blocks push it up (by at
most 12.5% per block); empty blocks pull it down.

<Callout type="insight" title="±12.5% is a speed limit, not a target">
The base fee can change by at most one-eighth per block. That cap is why a sudden
demand spike doesn't instantly 10× your fee — it ramps over several blocks,
giving you time to react. Sustained full blocks compound it (1.125ⁿ), so a long
congestion event *does* get expensive — just predictably so.
</Callout>

## Grounded in the spec

The controller is a few lines of integer math. Each block's base fee is computed
purely from its parent:

<SpecExcerpt
  source="EIP-1559: Fee market change"
  href="https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1559"
  ref="base fee calculation"
>
```python
# parent over target → base fee rises (capped at +1/8); under → falls
if parent_gas_used == parent_gas_target:
    base_fee = parent_base_fee
elif parent_gas_used > parent_gas_target:
    delta   = parent_base_fee * (parent_gas_used - parent_gas_target) \
              // parent_gas_target // BASE_FEE_MAX_CHANGE_DENOMINATOR   # = 8
    base_fee = parent_base_fee + max(delta, 1)
else:
    delta   = parent_base_fee * (parent_gas_target - parent_gas_used) \
              // parent_gas_target // BASE_FEE_MAX_CHANGE_DENOMINATOR
    base_fee = parent_base_fee - delta
```
</SpecExcerpt>

What a transaction actually pays per gas is then
`min(maxFeePerGas, baseFee + maxPriorityFeePerGas)` — the base fee (burned) plus
the smaller of your tip and the headroom left under your fee cap.

<Callout type="note" title="maxFee vs maxPriorityFee">
You sign two ceilings: `maxFeePerGas` (the absolute most you'll pay per gas) and
`maxPriorityFeePerGas` (the most you'll tip). If the base fee rises above your
`maxFeePerGas`, your transaction simply isn't includable until the base fee falls
— it sits in the mempool rather than overpaying. Set the cap high, the tip
modest, and 1559 handles the rest.
</Callout>

</DepthLayer>

## The tip is for ordering — and that opens a market

The base fee gets you *includable*; the tip competes for *position*. And position
has value: the order of transactions in a block can be worth money (a liquidation
to capture, an arbitrage to front-run). That value is
<KeyTerm term="MEV" def="Maximal Extractable Value — the profit a block builder can extract by choosing which transactions to include and in what order. It turned block-building into a specialized, competitive market." />, and
it reshaped block production.

Today most proposers don't build their own blocks. Under
**proposer-builder separation (PBS)**, specialized *builders* assemble the most
valuable block they can (mempool transactions + private order flow, optimally
ordered) and bid for the proposer to publish it. The proposer just picks the
highest bid. The mempool is no longer the only door into a block.

That hand-off — from your signed transaction, through the mempool, to a built and
proposed block — is where the execution layer meets consensus. Which is exactly
where we go next.
